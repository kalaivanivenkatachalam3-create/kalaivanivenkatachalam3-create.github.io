Problem

Policy changes were difficult to detect early and consistently.

Payer policy updates appeared across portals, bulletins, PDFs and HTML pages. Manual monitoring was time-consuming and vulnerable to coverage gaps. When changes were missed or identified late, claims could be submitted using outdated or non-compliant rules, creating denial, hold and rework risk.

The product’s north-star outcome is denial reduction. The MVP itself was designed first to prove reliable capture, review workflow fit and proactive detection before claiming direct denial impact.

My role

Turning a technical AI exercise into a business-aligned product.

I owned product strategy and delivery across discovery, roadmap, requirements, UI workflow, business validation and readiness for scale. I worked with business users to understand how they judged relevance and billing actionability, then translated that decision model into product requirements.

What I did
1 · Clarified the decision model

Separated Billing vs Others, then Actionable vs Not Actionable for Billing.

2 · Improved ontology

Worked with business stakeholders on signals such as reimbursement logic, denial language, claim requirements, code context, frequency limits, site-of-service, effective dates and change language.

3 · Built evidence into the workflow

Introduced human-readable reasoning, historical records/golden datasets, user feedback and evaluation before broader payer scale.

Measurement

Quality first, outcome attribution second.

99%Average PDF recall across the UHC and Cigna pilots (98% and 100% documented).
17%Early business-confirmed classification accuracy snapshot — kept separate from recall.

For the longer-term business outcome, I would measure case volume against human-identified policy changes and establish a validated denial baseline and attribution model. That keeps internal AI quality metrics distinct from the eventual business outcome.

Outcome & learning

Trust became a product requirement, not just a model metric.

The work moved the product toward a two-stage, evidence-backed classification model with feedback and observability. The biggest learning was that AI product discovery is not only prompt tuning — it is discovering the business decision model and making it explainable enough for users to trust.

Product takeaway

For AI products, the hard part is often not model capability — it is discovering the business decision model, making it explainable, and proving trust before scale.
