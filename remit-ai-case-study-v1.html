Problem

Traditional portal automation was difficult to scale across highly variable payer sites.

A/R follow-up depended on payer-portal interactions such as ERA/EOB retrieval and claim-status checks. Portals varied in UI structure, credentials, MFA, access restrictions and automation constraints, making traditional automation expensive to scale and maintain.

My role

I focused on where Agentic AI could create business value — and where it should not be used.

I owned product prioritization, POC definition, success criteria, rollout thinking and stakeholder alignment, while partnering with engineering on the technical solution. I treated feasibility, compliance, reliability and economics as product decisions.

What I did
Prioritized the POC

Started with ERA downloads and deliberately selected technically diverse portals rather than only easy cases.

Defined boundaries

Separated non-automatable portals caused by compliance, CAPTCHA, authenticator apps, access limitations and other constraints.

Improved reliability

Supported product prioritization around DOM grounding, portal-specific functions, custom labeling and richer context to reduce hallucination risk.

Scaled the lifecycle

Explored Prompt Pilot and Prompt Refiner approaches to reduce the effort and cycle time of portal onboarding and integration testing.

Evaluated economics

Assessed model cost alongside reliability and consistency; evaluated GPT-5 mini plus context summarization as a potential lower-cost path.

Outcome & learning

Scaling AI required a product system around the agent.

The work demonstrated that Agentic AI was not simply a model choice. It required portal prioritization, deterministic browser capabilities where appropriate, grounding, reliability controls, human review, onboarding mechanisms and economics.

Product takeaway

Successful Agentic AI is not just an agent that works. It is a repeatable product system for prioritization, reliability, onboarding, governance and economics.
