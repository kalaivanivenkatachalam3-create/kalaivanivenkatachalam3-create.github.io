Problem

Healthcare RCM workflows were repetitive, time-consuming and fragmented.

Many RCM workflows were manual or supported by fragmented automations. The result was cost and quality pressure, along with a growing need for a more reliable operating model as automation expanded.

My role

I treated automation as a product and platform problem.

I worked with business teams to identify automation-fit workflows, prioritize opportunities, define MVP boundaries and shape the operating model. As the footprint grew, I focused on standardization, reliability, observability, governance and modernization.

What I did
Discover & prioritize

Mapped repetitive RCM workflows, evaluated business value and automation fit, and aligned teams on what to automate.

Standardize operations

Created checklists for maintenance, deployments and patching; established vendor health reviews, SOPs, knowledge base practices, incident management and on-call support.

Strengthen reliability

Supported HA/backup readiness, alerting and monitoring, xMatters escalation, incident/postmortem processes and actionable recovery practices.

Modernize the platform

Introduced machine puppetization for uniform configuration, centralized logging with OpenSearch, Grafana and Power BI reporting, and explored in-house Java/Selenium and Node.js-based automation.

Productize as a service

Shaped an internal automation gateway for credential-management pilots: APIs + database + scheduled processing, with inputs from a partner product team and outputs returned as a service.

Plan the end state

The modernization path ultimately included decommissioning the legacy automation platform after establishing the replacement foundation.

Security & architecture

Build for enterprise constraints, not just happy-path automation.

For productized automation services, features passed security checks including threat modelling and closure of resulting actions. The service was deployed in AWS using ECS, with the gateway/API, database and scheduled processing supporting the workflow.

My PM contribution was defining the service need, workflow, inputs/outputs, acceptance criteria and cross-functional readiness; engineering owned the detailed implementation.

Outcome & learning

Reliability became part of the product experience.

The platform work created a more standardized and observable operating model while the automation portfolio delivered measurable savings. The key learning was that scaling automation requires an operating system around the bots — governance, reliability, monitoring, support, security and eventually a path to retire the underlying legacy dependency.

Product takeaway

Automation only scales when the platform around it scales: standards, reliability, observability, support, security and a clear modernization path.
